Sound Money and Stamp Collecting

God cannot change the past anymore, only the historians can.
Samuel Buttler (1612-1680)

It is well known that the Swiss currency, the Swiss Franc (CHF), is one of the most stable currencies in the world. The reason for this is that an article of the Swiss Constitution still imposes to the government to cover with gold an percentage of the money being issued. That’s why the Swiss central bank permanently keeps an important quantity of gold in its deposits.

Recently, mostly under the pressure of the welfare-state advocates, the Swiss government charged a commission with the task to give its opinion on the necessity to maintain the actual coverage in gold of the CHF. The commission answered that:

  • The constitutional article demanding the gold backing of money is presently obsolete 
  • The backing of the currency with gold has become outmoded, it is not needed anymore and should be abolished from the constitution and from praxis (it was eliminated later from the Constitution).

Nothing unusual for the representatives of an economical and philosophical thought that, unfortunately, dominate the present academia! The first results of that answer are that:

  • The price of gold already dropped on the stock exchange
  • A big battle started in Switzerland and worldwide between different lobbies in order to get the biggest possible share of this unexpected, but so much desired wealth (which is, actually, the property of the Swiss nation as a whole).

To get a better understanding of the issue, let's see what a well known economist and later statesman, Mr. Alan Greenspan, wrote in his article Gold and Economic Freedom. (1):"… the opposition to the gold standard in any form – from a growing number of welfare-advocates – was prompted by a much subtler insight: the realization that the gold standard is incompatible with chronic deficit spending (the hallmark of the welfare state). Stripped of its academic jargon, the welfare state is nothing more than a mechanism by which governments confiscate the wealth of the productive members of a society to support a wide variety of welfare schemes. A substantial part of the confiscation is effected by taxation. But welfare statists were quickly to recognize that if they wished to retain political power, the amount of taxation had to be limited and they had to resort to programs of massive deficit spending, i.e., they have to borrow money, by issuing governments bonds, to finance welfare expenditures on a large scale.

Under a gold standard (or even under the mix of today, with some remnants of the old gold standard in place – V.M.), the amount of credit that an economy can support is determined by the economy’s tangible assets, since every credit instrument is ultimately a claim on some tangible asset. But government bonds are not backed by tangible wealth, only by government’s promise out of future tax revenues, and cannot easily be absorbed by the financial markets. A large volume of new government bonds can be sold to the public only at progressively higher interest rates. Thus, government deficit spending under a gold standard is severely limited".

The question you, the reader of this column, can ask is: Does all this stuff have anything to do with our hobby, stamps collecting? The first, short answer is: for sure it has, and a lot. The second short answer is: it has still much more to do with our life and liberty. Below I will shortly address only the first aspect, the consequences it has on philately.

Have you ever asked yourself the question why the new issues from countries like the USA, Switzerland or Germany are considered by most of the philatelists as more serious, compared to the issues of former socialist countries, Paraguay, Guyana and a lot of other countries? And why old issues of wealthy countries have higher market prices, even if less rare (for example, the 1845 Basle Dove is more expensive to get than the Moldova 1858 27p, even if the later is really very rare)?

Well, the obvious answer is that, because the stamps have a market value, they are strongly related to money and to economy generally. The sounder the economical politics of a country are, the wealthier its citizens are, the more credible its currency is and the wealthier buyers it has for its stamps (older or newer ones). Therefore all stamps collectors have a strong interest to preserve the free market, a limited government and sound money politics. As Mr. Greenspan stated in his youth, it is possible only if governmental expenses are controlled, what can only be achieved by backing the currency with a real and universal asset, i.e. gold.
