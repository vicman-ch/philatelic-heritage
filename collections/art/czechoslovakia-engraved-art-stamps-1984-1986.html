Czechoslovakia · The art of engraving

Czechoslovakia – Engraved Art Stamps, 1984–1986

From painting to manuscript illumination. The beauty of these miniature reproductions lies in their interplay of fine engraved lines, colour and carefully balanced compositions. The original margins and perforations are preserved in the illustrations.

1984

Four individual stamps with adjoining margins show the variety of the art series: a wooded landscape by Karel Štěch, a gathering of figures, a vivid composition of red trees and an illuminated page from the Vyšehrad Codex.

Karel Štěch (1912–1979)

A Czech painter and graphic artist, Štěch is represented here by an autumnal woodland landscape. Fine lines articulate the branches against the warm colours of the foliage.

Viktor Barvitius (1834–1902)

A Czech painter associated with realism, Barvitius depicted contemporary urban and social life. The gathering of figures here rewards close inspection of the costumes and gestures.

Zolo Palugyay (1898–1935)

The Slovak modernist painter used expressive colour and simplified forms. Red trees and rhythmic contours dominate this landscape.

The Vyšehrad Codex (1085)

This Romanesque illuminated manuscript is associated with the coronation of Vratislav II. The stamp reproduces the richly patterned geometry and vivid colours of medieval book illumination.

1986

The sheets bring together contrasting subjects, from a brightly coloured decorative figure to portraits, architecture and classical figures. The arrangement of four impressions makes the fine lines and tonal contrasts particularly easy to compare.

Vratislav Hugo Brunner (1886–1928)

A Czech illustrator, graphic artist and book designer, Brunner brought decorative invention to printed art. Here the intricate figure contrasts with a brilliant red ground.

Jozef Šturdík (1920–1992)

The Slovak painter and graphic artist is represented by a seated figure. The dark clothing and restrained background emphasize the face and hands.

František Gross (1909–1985)

A Czech painter associated with Group 42, Gross explored the modern urban environment. Architectural forms and linear structures give this composition its distinctive character.

Lucas Cranach the Elder (1472–1553)

A leading German Renaissance painter, Cranach developed a distinctive treatment of the human figure. The slender figures and dark background are characteristic of his pictorial language.

Karel Škréta (1610–1674)

A major Czech Baroque painter, Škréta worked in portraiture and religious painting. The dramatic contrast of light and shadow is particularly effective in this miniature reproduction.

El Greco

A sheet of four stamps reproduces a devotional portrait attributed in the supplied material to El Greco. The issue date is not established here.

El Greco (1541–1614)

Born in Crete and active in Spain, El Greco developed an expressive style of elongated forms and spiritual intensity. The face and luminous colour are central to this image.
