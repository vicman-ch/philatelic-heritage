The story behind the website · Since March 1997

From a 56K Modem to a Museum of Art in Miniature

Art on Stamps began in March 1997, when the World Wide Web was still young and sharing a collection of postage stamps online was an adventure in patience, ingenuity and enthusiasm.

A collection finds a new audience

In March 1997, I decided to bring selections from my extensive collection of art-related postage stamps to the Web. Philatelic websites were still in their infancy, and the idea of presenting miniature works of art to visitors around the world was as exciting as it was technically challenging.

When every minute online mattered

My connection was through a 56 kbps dial-up modem, linked to a local server over the telephone line. Going online meant watching the clock: telephone charges were relatively high, so I connected, did what I needed to do, and disconnected as quickly as possible.

Today's visitors can scarcely imagine how much those limitations influenced the design of an illustrated website.

Scanning stamps by hand

Each stamp or miniature sheet had to be scanned individually. I used a handheld Logitech scanner, moving it carefully across the surface. It was a slow process, and there was always the risk of damaging the very items I wanted to share.

With ten to fifteen scans on a single page, image resolution had to remain modest. Every illustration represented a compromise between visual quality and loading time, compounded by the limited screen resolution of the monitors of that period.

Better tools, the same fascination

Only gradually could I improve the quality and dimensions of the images. Eventually, the handheld scanner gave way to a stationary one, making the work easier and allowing more faithful reproductions.

Why the early scans remain

I have deliberately not replaced the early scans on this historical website with sharper modern versions. Their modest resolution is part of the site's story. They recall the pioneering years when I first began sharing my admiration for the tiny works of art reproduced on postage stamps.

The images may be small, but they preserve something larger: the spirit of discovery that inspired this website in March 1997.

Art on Stamps is therefore more than a collection of illustrated pages. It is also a record of how a collector's enthusiasm met the possibilities—and limitations—of the early Web.

Written in the first person from the website founder's recollections. Illustrations are examples from the Art on Stamps collection, not photographs of the original scanning equipment.
