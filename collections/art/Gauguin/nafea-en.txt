When will you marry? (Tahitian: Nafea faa ipoipo?, French: Quand te maries-tu?) is an oil painting from 1892 by the French Post-Impressionist artist Eugène Henri Paul Gauguin.

Gauguin travelled to Tahiti in 1891 for the first time. Here he never learned to speak the Tahitian language well. The painting When will you marry? shows, however, that his art evolved consistently in dealing with the foreign. He came close to the native women and in his paintings he placed them in the wild, naked or dressed in traditional Tahitian clothes, although their colonist leaders forced them to wear missionary dresses.

The front and middle ground are built up in areas of green, yellow and blue. Behind lies the tropical landscape, painted in very intense, glowing colours. A traditionally dressed woman has settled on the threshold between the front and middle ground. Behind her rises abruptly a second figure in a high-necked mission dress. The gesture of her hand commands attention. The contradictions are clear: While the front figure indulges in dreamy fantasies, the rear figure is imbued with something rigid and rule-bound. The front woman stretches herself, her facial features stylized and simplified. The rear female figure is flush with the yellow-blue area. Her face is painted with individual features and represents the center of the imager. The pink colour of her dress is clearly distinct from the other colours.

The two women may reflect Gauguin's own situation as caught between the artist's world and the world of the women, between tradition and the foreign, wishes and rules.

It is unknown who asks the question "When are you getting married?" and whether it is an expression or an invitation.
