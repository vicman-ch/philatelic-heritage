Editorial: Che Guevara can suck it
by Nick Wilson


“[It is] relentless hatred of the enemy that impels us over and beyond the natural limitations of man and transforms us into effective, violent, selective, and cold killing machines.” - Che Guevara

Some things are just unexplainable. Crop circles. UFO sightings. Alien abductions. Michael Jackson.

Even more unexplainable is exactly how so-called “progressives” across the globe have immortalized Che Guevara and whitewashed his legacy, turning him into a romantic martyr, a Jesus-like figure who sacrificed his life for a hopeless cause and a higher ideal. He’s also been a boon for those who sell his sex symbol image to upper middle class collegiate progressives willing to fork $20 into the “capitalist machine” in order to wear a shirt showing how radical and anti-capitalist they are.

I find it rather frightening that the clichéd mythologization of a totalitarian mass murderer pervades Warren Wilson. In a college that prides itself for its openmindedness and commitment to peace, justice and the environment, it seems as though we should hate a man who set up gulags that housed dissidents, Catholic priests and gays, who supported the mass state industrialization of Cuba, who received personal enjoyment out of executing dissidents, who supported dictatorial power over democratization, and who, far more than Castro or Khrushchev, supported turning the Cuban missile crisis into a real nuclear holocaust.

Although I am a progressive, I am staunchly anti-communist, so I hold no ideological affinity with Che Guevara. Communism can not exist outside of theory without totalitarianism, without purging the dissidents and undesirables from society and without the massive state deprogramming and conditioning of human nature.

I have no problem with revolution in and of itself. The previous dictator of Cuba, General Fulgencio Batista, deserved to be overthrown, and U.S. foreign policy in Central and South America has long needed to be changed. However, I have a serious problem with replacing one oppressive dictator with another.

It’s always fascinating to watch the oppressed become the oppressor. It happened in Nazi Germany, the Soviet Union, China and Cuba. Guevara himself couldn’t have said it better: “Cruel leaders are replaced only to have new leaders turn cruel!” It’s also fascinating to watch leftist dissidents in capitalist countries herald Stalin, Lenin, Mao, Guevara and Castro, all of whom suppressed dissidence in their own countries.

Che went from idealist to cold-hearted brute over the course of the Cuban revolution. Guevara personally oversaw the execution of thousands of “enemies of the cause,” and has been quoted numerous times saying that it gave him great personal pleasure. He was also responsible for establishing Cuban labor camps for those he didn’t execute. By the mid-1960s, these camps also housed gays, Catholic priests and other social “undesirables” unfit for society or military service.

As Minister of Industry, Che pushed a massive industrialization scheme (which failed), supporting widespread state construction of high-polluting thermoelectric plants, refineries and factories. Fast forward forty years, and Che shirt-wearing anarchists and environmentalists take to the streets, expounding the social and environmental evils of industrialization. Oh, the irony.

Those same Che shirt-wearing radicals shake their fists at the nuclear weapons arsenal of America. Unfortunately, its people like Che who force us to have such an arsenal. During the Cuban missile crisis, Che was the biggest advocate for Cuba actually pushing the button. “We must proceed along the path of liberation even if that costs millions of atomic victims,” Che said in an article written during the crisis. When the strike didn’t happen, Che was crestfallen, eventually leading to his disenchantment with Castro and Khrushchev.

Che brought the world as close to actual nuclear warfare as it has ever been – the US considered launching nukes at both Cuba and the USSR – and probably would have, had the situation escalated further. Cuba would be a hole in the ocean had Che gotten his way. There would be sizable holes in the US and the USSR as well, and many of us would not be here. Then again, I guess Che’s “cause” would have been furthered by the mutual deaths of millions of evil American civilians and all the poor Cubans he fought for. Che told the Latin American Youth Congress in 1959: “These people [of Cuba] you see today tell you that even if they should disappear from the face of the earth because an atomic war is unleashed in their names ...they would feel completely happy and fulfilled.”

I don’t have space for a deeper analysis of the gigantic, ironic inconsistencies between Che and the ideals of his shirtwearers, but I implore you to be fully aware of the true historical impact of this man beyond the one-sided rhetoric, “The Motorcycle Diaries,” the pop culture idolatry, the sex appeal, the romanticism of his revolutionary zeal and his successful quest for martyrdom. History is easily whitewashed. Just because someone is idealistic, had great quotes, died romantically and fought unwaveringly against some percieved injustice does not make them worthy of immortalization. Hitler fits the criteria too, but thank God nobody’s wearing his face on a shirt because it’s fashionable.
