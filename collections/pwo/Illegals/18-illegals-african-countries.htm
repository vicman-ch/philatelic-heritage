WORLDWIDE PHILATELIC OBSERVATORY - An Activity of Progress Market Research (PEM), Tunisia

Subject. Promoting and Protecting the culture and heritage of the African countries through their postage stamps and taking measures to combat the production and distribution of illegal stamps in the names of the 52 African countries concerned.

Postage Stamps, as Ambassadors and miniature works of art and promoters of their Nation and its cultural heritage, are by their very nature an added value to each Nation and an important element to be included in the Charter for African Cultural Renaissance. The Africa we want that is part of the objectives of the African Union. The Charter focuses on cultural diversity, identity and renaissance and the need to assert the dignity of African people and the African Diaspora. A well-thought out and planned stamp issuing programme and its promotion are a valuable asset to each Nation.

Moreover, the role of postage stamps is vital in «reconstructing and teaching the history of Africa and the African Diaspora». These concepts are also part of the Charter for African Cultural Renaissance. Stamps are widely recognized as an educational tool and are part of school curriculum and higher studies in certain countries. The African Diaspora can easily be kept informed of its countries' traditions and current role in the world through the medium of postage stamps.

Postage stamps are not only the «Ambassadors» of their nation, but they are also important sources of revenue, both nationally and internationally, not only for the postal service but for the country's economy. Postage stamps are a rare national product that brings in foreign currency from sales at exhibitions and trade fairs around the world. Consequently, it is all the more important that all measures be taken to safeguard the postage stamps for the benefit of the countries. The need to protect the postage stamp is strengthened by the fact that 52 out of the 54 countries comprising the African continent are today victims of the production and distribution of illegal stamps in their names. Certain countries have been victims since the early 2000s and one can easily imagine all the damage to the images of the countries concerned and the potential revenue that has been lost because of stamp collectors purchasing illegal stamps instead of the genuine stamps. It is even more reprehensible that pornography and sexually explicit scenes should be reproduced on what are being identified as «stamps» in the name of various African countries. Countries of other regions in the world are also similarly affected.

The Southern African region that is comprised of 15 countries within the SADC has unfortunately 14 of its member countries suffering from the plague of the illegal stamps. See the attached map as well as the presentation that was made on the occasion of a special philately workshop for the countries of the Southern Africa region that was organised in Pretoria in October 2014.

The 7 and 8 March International Conference of the South African Cultural Observatory can address the need for urgent actions to be taken to safeguard the cultural identity of the African countries concerned against the proliferation of illegal stamps. A call for actions should be submitted to the Panafrican Postal Union and the World Association for the Development of Philately of the Universal Postal Union.

Already, the Worldwide Philatelic Observatory (WPO) that is based in Tunisia is mobilizing partners from around the world to address the serious attack on the cultural identity of the countries concerned from the production and distribution of illegal stamps in their name.
