Dear Mr. Delcampe,
 
We are glad to finally hear from you again on the painful subject that we discussed several times. 
 
First of all, we disagree with your terminology, which can be misleading. "The fictitious modern prints sold to philatelists (especially thematics)", as you call them, are not a subdivision of "Cinderellas". They were designated by the Universal Postal Union as "illegal stamps", a term that correctly denotes their illegal status.
 
 See on this subject for example Wikipedia:
 
"Illegal stamps are postage stamp-like labels issued in the names of existing independent countries or territories, entities claiming to be independent, or completely fictitious entities. They are a subset of philatelic fakes and forgeries for which a member nation of the Universal Postal Union (UPU) has caused the issue of an "International Bureau Circular" advising others of this label. 
Unlike (emphasis ours) Cinderella stamps these labels may be used to defraud postal administrations, stamp collectors, and the general public, but some are used to fulfill actual domestic postal needs of unrecognized entities, and a few are nothing more than propaganda labels. According to the UPU, the market is estimated to be at least $500 million per year."
http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_stamps 

As you know from the messages that we sent to you previously, the PWO and the stamp collectors connected to it denounced many times to you the illegal stamps sold on your website. Just to give you a crying, actual example, a search on your site after "kalmykia"  produced not less than 239 proposed sales, see:

http://www.delcampe.com/list.php?language=E&searchString=kalmykia&cat=0&searchMode=all&searchTldCountry=com&searchInDescription=N

It is general knowledge that such stamps are illegals, being denounced in 2010 by the Russian Federation to the UPU, as mentioned in the UPU IB Circular 287, see:
http://www.upu.int/document/2010/an/bi_circulaires/287.pdf

Please notice that your assertion: "Presently, selling (legal) Cinderellas is allowed on our site http://www.delcampe.net, as long as “CINDERELLA” is clearly written in the title." doesn't apply to them, they don't being marked as Cinderella...

Most of these kind of stamps are quite easy to identify, they are obviously "violating the rights of others", and for these reasons we expect that you apply to them the strict policy that you mention in your message, by forbidding their future sales and by eliminating the existing ones from your site. 

We hope to learn from you soon that this policy was consistently implemented throughout your site(s).

Hoping that they can help you better than us in your attempt, we have allowed ourselves to send this message to UPU and AIJP. As far as we know, your attempt is the first of this kind, and one that would give an excellent example to other important stamp selling sites worldwide. For this reason we hope that you will get a full support from WADP (UPU and AIJP being two of its members) in your struggle against the illegals.

Amicalement,

Victor Manta, PWO, AIJP
PWO President

Dear Sir, Dear Madam,

I contact you because we are now gathering opinions about “Cinderellas sold on Internet”. As you probably know it, the Delcampe site tends to be as ethical as possible in all its activities, and we are concerned by this thread for philately.

Presently, selling (legal) Cinderellas is allowed on our site http://www.delcampe.net, as long as “CINDERELLA” is clearly written in the title. This was a first step to have things cleaner, but we think it is now time to go a bit further.  

First we tried to define what Cinderellas are. It seems to be clear that the Cinderellas are defined primarily by what they are not! These are labels not issued by a postal authority, instead of postage stamps.  

We split the Cinderellas in two broad categories; 

1.       The fictitious modern prints sold to philatelists (especially thematics) 
2.       Other (revenue stamps, exhibitions, tourism promotion, charity, etc..)  

The first of these two broad categories concerns us most. We sub-categorize as follows: 

-          Design including the name of a territory (country, region) that has never existed; 
-          Design including the name of a territory (country, region) that exists or has existed, but has never issued stamps; 
-          Design showing the name of a territory (country, region) that exists or has existed, and which has issued stamps. 

The trouble about them is mainly: 

-          That they can be sold without the buyer being aware that these are not postage stamps; 
-          They can go against the rights of individuals, countries, companies or organizations in general (use of logos, names of countries, drawings, etc..).  

Here's what we plan, to regulate the sale of these cinderellas: 

1.       For all: the seller must write "CINDERELLA" in block letters, as first word of the title, and place the sale in a new category "Stamps > Themes > Modern Cinderellas" to the exclusion of all others. 
2.       For those violating the rights of others, they are clearly forbidden and we prepare a program of protection of rights on our site (even in a system beyond the limits of Cinderella).  

We believe that these decisions are sound and will allow all, for or against the (legal) Cinderella's, continue to find their happiness on our site. 

We solicit your opinion on this, only some lines would be perfect. Your precious experience, and competency, may bring a good vision that can help us to have things cleaner in our site, so in Internet sales. We think this is important for all actors of philately.

We are now contacting you among several competent people of the philatelic world. After July 15th, we will take decisions thanks to the replies we will have received, but a late reply is of course also possible. 

Thank you in advance for the possible help you can give us. 

Best regards, 

Seb Delcampe
